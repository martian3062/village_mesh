VILLAGE_MESH — VERSION 2 HYPERREALISM PLAN
Pandori Nijjaran, Punjab
Prepared: 23 October 2026 (IST)
Basis: README(20261023-131009).md and VALIDATION.md

This is a proposed implementation plan. It does not report new engine tests,
measured FPS, inspected screenshots, or completed changes. The two supplied
documents were reviewed; repository code and generated Unreal assets were
not available for a line-by-line or visual audit.

1. RECOMMENDATION

Keep the existing geographic foundation and Unreal pipeline. Make V2 a
reference-built, living village with convincing street-level detail.

The largest gains should come from:
- correct proportions and ground levels;
- recognizable local architecture and materials;
- coherent lighting, surface response and weather;
- believable people, sound and interactions;
- stable rendering and streaming during movement.

The project already has Lumen, Substrate, Nanite foliage, weather and physics.
More enabled plugins alone will not supply the missing observations.

Build one excellent 150–250 m street first, then extend its reusable
architecture and placement rules. Aim for realism at walking distance before
increasing the map area. A visually convincing reconstruction and a surveyed
digital twin are different deliverables; label measured and inferred details.

2. WHAT V1 ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES

The supplied documents record:
- A finite 1.28 km × 1.28 km world, approximately 1.6384 square kilometres.
- 821 mapped building shells, grouped into 265 spatial/material mesh chunks.
- 306 base actors, plus 399 procedural roof-tank/shade detail actors.
- 146 vegetation instances and 13 physics props in the recorded run.
- Windows editor build on Unreal Engine 5.8.3 with MSVC 14.44.
- An RTX 4050 Laptop GPU runtime smoke check.
- Seven Python unit tests and a dependency audit of 1,026 packages.
- World Partition conversion, with HLOD baking still unfinished.
- A pinned 49-sample, normalized and retimed Well wind playback signal.

These results establish a functioning foundation. They do not establish:
- surveyed terrain, building heights or facade accuracy;
- realistic interiors or integrated villagers in the portable explorer;
- long-duration frame rate, memory stability or complete traversal;
- an independently tested packaged game;
- scientifically validated local atmosphere or water simulation.

The 821 shells are not 821 detailed houses or necessarily 821 separate actors.
The reported player Z position is a runtime grounding observation, not a terrain
survey result. Preserve these distinctions in the V2 documentation.

3. PROPOSED V2 RELEASE SCOPE

These counts are planning choices, not engine requirements or achieved results.

Detailed pilot:
- One reference-backed street, approximately 150–250 m long.
- Twelve to twenty reviewed frontages.
- Two or three enterable spaces: for example, a courtyard, a shop and a room.
- One locally verified distinctive site or building, selected after reference
  collection rather than invented from a map label.
- A small kit of roughly 20–30 useful props and architectural components.
- A first runtime population test of 6–10 nearby villagers, 2–4 animals and
  one vehicle route. Reduce or expand after profiling.
- Four presentation conditions: clear daylight, evening, overcast and rain.
- A tested packaged explorer and a separate capture-quality preset.

Rest of the village:
- Preserve the mapped layout.
- Improve silhouette, road edges, field boundaries and facade variation.
- Use cheaper background buildings and distant proxies.
- Upgrade additional streets only after the pilot meets fidelity and
  performance gates.

If local reference collection is unavailable, V2 can still become a plausible
Punjab-inspired environment. Exact Pandori fidelity remains unverified until
the relevant local observations are obtained.

4. DATA AND REFERENCE ACQUISITION

4.1 Local reference pack — highest priority

For the pilot street, collect:
- Front-facing and oblique facade images at known viewpoints.
- Roofline, parapet, gate, window, balcony, stair and plinth dimensions.
- Street widths at intersections and regular cross-sections.
- Relative ground levels at drains, road crown, door thresholds and courtyards.
- Surface close-ups with a scale reference: brick, plaster, metal, paving, soil.
- Tree placement, canopy size, field boundaries and the season represented.
- Short ambient recordings where appropriate.
- Date, approximate time, camera orientation and exposure information.

Use images and recordings you are entitled to use. For private interiors,
obtain permission or make a clearly labelled representative interior.
Do not reproduce identifiable people or private-house details without the
appropriate permission.

A phone GPS position alone does not establish centimetre survey accuracy.
For precise geometry, use suitable measured control, survey equipment or
photogrammetry with scale and independent check dimensions.

Proposed reference manifest fields:
feature_id, provider_id, capture_date, source, permission_or_license,
capture_position, orientation, measured_dimensions, units, uncertainty,
reference_paths, confidence, reviewer, notes.

Use explicit confidence categories:
- measured;
- photo-estimated;
- sourced map attribute;
- procedural inference.

4.2 Audit the existing geodata before downloading another release

Overture supports optional height, floor-count, building-part and facade/roof
attributes. Inspect whether those fields are actually populated for these
821 buildings. Preserve useful values and their provenance; missing values
must remain distinguishable from measurements. Footprints may be roofprints,
so compare them with ground-level walls and setbacks. [S1]

Keep a stable internal building identifier and an explicit mapping to source
IDs/releases. A source refresh must not silently delete authored corrections.

4.3 Terrain: broad relief plus measured street detail

Copernicus GLO-30 is a global 30 m digital surface model that includes buildings
and vegetation; its principal acquisition dates are 2011–2015. It can help
establish broad context after inspection, but it is not a current bare-earth
survey or a source of street-level drains. Interpolation cannot create missing
measured detail. [S2]

Use a suitable local terrain survey or scaled photogrammetry for the pilot.
Where this is unavailable, author plausible microterrain and mark it inferred.

Record horizontal CRS, vertical datum, origin and unit conversions. Retain the
existing east/north/up convention unless a deliberate migration is tested.
Convert metres to Unreal centimetres once, at a defined import boundary.
Do not combine unrelated elevation datums without accounting for the offset.

4.4 Selective photogrammetry

Use RealityScan on surfaces or objects that benefit from unique geometry:
textured wall sections, steps, gate surrounds, roadside stones and props.
Capture overlapping views at several heights; missing views leave missing
information. Establish scale, clean the mesh, remove unwanted background,
inspect lighting baked into textures and build appropriate collision. [S3]

RealityScan 2.2, released 24 June 2026, adds AMD/mixed-GPU support and describes
an experimental 360-camera workflow. It is an option for capture work, not
evidence of a speedup on this project's RTX 4050. [S4]

For repeated house components, a measured modular kit will usually be easier
to maintain than an enormous raw scan. Test one scan and one modular facade
in-engine before expanding capture.

4.5 Land cover

ESA WorldCover provides 10 m products for 2020 and 2021. Use them only for broad
land-cover context. They do not establish present-day individual tree positions,
crop species, narrow paths or current field boundaries. [S5]

5. BUILDINGS, ROADS AND THE GROUND

5.1 Replace generic facade generation with a reviewed local kit

Proposed kit:
- Exposed brick and plaster wall sections with appropriate thickness.
- Parapets, coping, roof edges and believable roof access.
- Gate leaves, frames, hinges and recessed entrances.
- Windows with reveals, grills, glass and interior depth.
- Stairs, plinths, thresholds, balconies and shaded porch components.
- Water tanks, pipes, outlets and mounting supports.
- Shop shutters and locally appropriate signage.
- Boundary walls and courtyard connections.

Derive dimensions and combinations from references. Review Gurmukhi signage
with a fluent reader. Avoid placing the same roof tank, window spacing and
decorative clutter on every house.

Hero facades need depth and silhouette: normal maps alone cannot replace a
window recess or a projecting parapet. Keep background detail cheaper.

5.2 Audit the current 265 mesh chunks

The README describes material/spatial grouping; actual chunk bounds and
lighting behaviour have not been inspected here.

Check whether any chunk joins complex or widely separated buildings. For
detailed buildings, separate walls, floors, ceilings and interaction parts
enough to support lighting, collision and streaming. Inspect Lumen Surface
Cache and distance-field views. Epic documents limitations for complex merged
interiors and thin surfaces. [S6]

Use instancing for repeated modules where it profiles well. Distant HLOD
merging is a separate decision from the structure used for nearby interiors.

5.3 Make entrances and interiors believable

For each enterable pilot space:
- Match the exterior opening to the interior floor plan and floor level.
- Include wall thickness, ceiling, functional threshold and collision.
- Add a modest, coherent set of furnishings.
- Implement door state and an intentional interaction.
- Test daylight entering the opening and exposure when crossing it.
- Preserve persistent door/prop state through streaming.

Use representative layouts when actual interiors have not been observed.
Do not imply that an invented room is the interior of a real resident's home.

5.4 Rebuild roads as surfaces with cross-sections

OSM centre lines do not describe the full visible road surface. Add measured
or explicitly inferred width, camber, shoulder, drainage, junction shapes and
material transitions.

Priorities:
1. Roads meet doors, lanes and courtyards at believable heights.
2. Intersections do not contain cracks, overlapping strips or abrupt steps.
3. Drains have plausible continuity and outfalls.
4. Potholes and repairs appear in context, rather than as uniform random noise.
5. Footpaths and narrow passages remain navigable.

Landscape splines, authored meshes and local height patches can be combined.
Choose the method according to the required geometry and observed performance.

6. MATERIALS AND SMALL-SCALE DETAIL

Build a small family of reusable materials rather than a unique expensive
shader for every object.

Add:
- Correct real-world texture scale.
- Consistent PBR import settings and channel interpretation.
- Roughness variation, chipped paint, repaired plaster and brick variation.
- Ground contact dirt, runoff below outlets and sheltered dry areas.
- A controlled decal set for cracks, stains, signs and repairs.
- Material blends between road, soil, paving and vegetation.

Use wear with a cause: moisture near drains, rust around exposed fixings,
dust where it collects, paint differences where surfaces were repaired.
Random grunge across every surface is visually repetitive.

Start with modest texture sizes for repeated pieces. Give higher resolution
to assets that occupy substantial screen space close to the camera. Review
texture streaming and memory before broadly increasing resolution.

Keep the existing Substrate setup initially. Add material complexity only
when a reference comparison shows a useful improvement. Test dry and wet
surfaces under neutral daylight before grading the final look.

7. LIGHTING, CAMERA AND ATMOSPHERE

Connect geographic sun positioning to the existing weather/light rig.
SunSky supports latitude, longitude, date, time zone and north alignment. [S7]

For this project's reference origin:
- Latitude: 31.4610594 N.
- Longitude: 75.7240654 E.
- Local civil time: Asia/Kolkata / UTC+05:30.
- Verify north against the project's X-east/Y-north convention.

Avoid introducing a second active sun or sky system when integrating SunSky.

Create repeatable lighting presets and fixed camera viewpoints. Compare
references at similar sun angle, view, exposure and season. Begin with neutral
colour and believable brightness; add artistic grading after the match works.

Review:
- Light leaks and excessively black interiors.
- Exposure adaptation when moving between sun and shade.
- Glass, water and rough-surface reflections.
- Contact shadows under props, walls and foliage.
- Volumetric haze and fog density.
- Temporal stability while walking and turning.

Use software Lumen as the initial playable baseline already established by
the project. Evaluate hardware ray tracing only as a separate measured profile.
Epic's published console frame budgets are not RTX 4050 laptop benchmarks. [S8]

Use a separate capture preset for expensive screenshots or offline sequences.
Capture quality must not be presented as the playable frame rate.

8. WEATHER, WATER AND PHYSICAL INTERACTION

8.1 One shared environment state

Extend WeatherManager around an explicit state:
time/date, sun state, wind vector in m/s, gust strength, rainfall rate,
cloudiness, wetness and drying parameters.

Document which values are physical quantities and which are artistic controls.
Drive foliage, rain direction, hanging cloth, dust, prop drag and audio from
the same state so they do not contradict one another.

8.2 Rain that interacts with the scene

Replace the camera streak shell as the sole rain representation with a
layered effect:
- Nearby rain, distant rain and splash effects.
- Shelter detection or authored shelter masks.
- Roof-edge runoff and downpipe outlets.
- Context-sensitive wetness and gradual drying.
- Limited puddles in appropriate depressions.
- Rain sound that changes when entering a building.

A Niagara system still needs authored collision, shelter and material logic.
Validate it in a covered porch, an indoor room and an exposed street.

8.3 Pond and drainage

For the pilot, use a coherent visual water system with shore interaction,
ripples and selected buoyant objects. Improve the existing plane approximation
only when an interaction exposes its limitation.

Do not put a full fluid solver across the entire map by default. Treat flood
modelling or hydrological prediction as a separate research task requiring
measured terrain, boundary conditions and validation.

8.4 Props and contact

Set mass, friction, restitution and collision according to the object's role.
Use sensible constraints for gates and hanging objects. Keep most scenery
static; activate simulation only for useful nearby interactions.

Test grabbing, throwing, resting, sleep/wake and streaming persistence.
Substepping and CCD help specific cases but do not guarantee realistic results
under long frame stalls.

8.5 Keep The Well contribution accurately described

The existing 49-sample trace is artistic wind modulation from 2D periodic
incompressible shear flow. It does not supply local wind shelter, vertical
structure or Punjab meteorology.

The official shear_flow page now documents an October 2026 v2 split correction
and warns that older benchmark scores used overlapping training/test data.
That does not invalidate a pinned visual playback. Keep the playback provenance;
if later training a model, use verified disjoint splits and independent
validation. Do not silently relabel the existing trace as the new dataset. [S9]

A sensible V2 wind upgrade is a coherent base wind plus locally authored
shelter/variation, explicitly described as an approximation.

9. LANDSCAPE, FIELDS AND VEGETATION

Replace uniform random placement with semantic areas:
road verges, courtyards, field edges, drainage edges and background vegetation.

Use native PCG with authored masks and rules for:
- Building/road exclusion and access clearance.
- Density by land use.
- Tree age, height and canopy variation.
- Crop rows, boundaries and seasonal state where supported by reference.
- Sparse weeds near appropriate edges.
- Fallen leaves and small ground detail close to the player.

Use local references to choose species and agricultural appearance. Two generic
vegetation assets cannot represent all local plant variation convincingly.

Place important trees manually from reference. Keep dense distant fields
cheap. Reduce foliage deformation, animation and shadow cost with distance
using supported settings, then verify the result in motion.

10. PEOPLE, ANIMALS, TRAFFIC AND SOUND

10.1 Believable routines first

Connect a small set of villagers to visible activities:
walking between places, sitting, talking, opening a shop, carrying an object,
or taking shelter when rain starts.

Use a schedule and StateTree/behaviour logic, navigation, and Smart Object
interaction slots. Smart Objects provide reservation and interaction data;
they do not implement the agent's actions automatically. [S10, S11]

Handle failed navigation, interrupted actions and released reservations.
Persist a lightweight identity/schedule while a person is far away; run
full animation and interaction only nearby.

A coherent routine with good animation will contribute more visible realism
than an unrestricted chatbot attached to an idle character.

10.2 Animation and animals

Start with clean locomotion, turns, stops, foot placement and hand-object
alignment. Add more elaborate motion systems only after the basic movement
works and licensed animation assets are available.

Animals need scale, movement, rest/feeding behaviour and appropriate sound.
Avoid identical timing and continuous random wandering.

10.3 Vehicles

Start with one low-density route and a measured interaction with pedestrians:
speed, turning, stopping, avoidance, parking and engine sound.
Choose a kinematic or physics-based vehicle according to whether the player
will drive or collide with it. Do not simulate every distant vehicle fully.

10.4 Audio

Add layered spatial sound:
- Distant ambience and nearby emitters.
- Footsteps by physical surface.
- Door, gate, prop and vehicle sounds.
- Birds, animals and foliage sound appropriate to the time and season.
- Indoor/outdoor attenuation and reverberation.
- Rain on different surfaces and muffling under shelter.

Use licensed recordings or your own. Audio should respond to the same weather
and activity state as the visible scene.

11. PERFORMANCE AND DELIVERY

11.1 Establish a baseline before adding density

Record the actual CPU, RAM, VRAM, driver, laptop power mode, storage and thermal
conditions. GPU model alone is insufficient to predict performance.

Use a cooked Development build for profiling, followed by a release-package
check. Keep editor-only Python/MCP authoring dependencies out of runtime paths.

Measure:
- Output resolution and internal render scale.
- CPU/game/render/GPU frame time.
- Median, p95 and p99 frame time; largest stalls.
- GPU and host memory peaks.
- Streaming, shader/PSO and asset-loading events.
- Cold launch and warm traversal separately.

11.2 Proposed targets, not promises

Initial playable target:
- Use the existing 1440 × 900 output as one reproducible baseline.
- Aim for p95 frame time at or below 33.3 ms after warmup on the tested laptop.
- Report p99 and maximum stalls, including any failures of this target.
- Treat a 16.7 ms / 60 fps profile as a later stretch goal.

These are proposed acceptance targets. No new FPS was measured for this plan.

Run at least three comparable traversal trials and one 30-minute session.
Do not hide a failing profile behind an average FPS number.

11.3 Finish HLODs and streaming

World Partition conversion does not generate finished HLOD proxies. Create
appropriate layers and build them. Epic documents instancing, merged and
simplified proxy options for representing unloaded cells. [S12]

Separate:
- Near-player detailed geometry and interactions.
- Mid-distance buildings and vegetation.
- Distant HLOD representation.

Test F9 overview without relying on loading the entire detailed village.
Confirm the loading range returns to its walking value afterward.

Avoid merging away interactive doors or hero interiors. Keep distant
simulation inexpensive. Use simple collision for assets that do not need
detailed contact.

11.4 Release discipline

Prove a clean packaged launch before expanding optional plugins. Test a fresh
machine or clean environment. Keep input manifests, seeds, generator version,
asset provenance and graphics settings with every published result.

13. MILESTONES AND EXIT CRITERIA

M0 — Reproducible baseline
Deliver:
Cooked pilot build, hardware/settings record, route and initial traces.
Exit:
Launch succeeds without editor authoring scripts; baseline measurements saved.

M1 — Grounded reference package
Deliver:
Pilot selection, photo viewpoints, dimensions, ground profiles and confidence
manifest; audit of available building attributes.
Exit:
Each hero feature has a traceable reference or a clearly labelled inference.

M2 — Geometry and architecture pilot
Deliver:
Corrected terrain/road interface, 12–20 frontages and reusable local kit.
Exit:
Reference comparisons show believable proportions; intended routes are
walkable; lighting representations are checked. Build initial HLOD proxies
and run the pilot traversal before expanding detail or adding population.

M3 — Finished street appearance
Deliver:
Materials, small props, fields/vegetation, calibrated sun and 2–3 interiors.
Exit:
Matched camera captures pass review in daylight, evening, overcast and wet
conditions; major light leaks and geometry intersections are resolved.
Repeat the initial streaming/performance gate before starting M4.

M4 — Living street
Deliver:
Small NPC/animal population, one vehicle route, spatial audio and useful
interactions.
Exit:
Routines, navigation, interaction reservations and rain responses work across
a complete demonstration cycle.

M5 — Streaming and optimization
Deliver:
Final HLOD rebuild and tuning, detail-distance rules, playable/capture presets
and runtime package. This extends the initial M2/M3 performance gates.
Exit:
The full route and overview work; performance is measured against the declared
target; memory stabilizes across repeated traversals.

M6 — V2 release evidence
Deliver:
Fresh-environment run, packaged explorer, reference comparisons, logs and
remaining-limitations document.
Exit:
No unresolved release-blocking crash, missing content or broken intended route;
claims match the recorded evidence.

Planning allowance:
A solo developer could reserve roughly 8–12 focused weeks for this bounded
pilot, depending heavily on reference access, art skills and reusable licensed
assets. This is an estimate, not a delivery commitment. Full-village close-up
fidelity is a later expansion and may take substantially longer.

14. V2 ACCEPTANCE MATRIX

Geography:
Compare independent check dimensions and control points, not only data used
to fit the model. Publish errors and uncertainty in metres. Set tolerances
according to the quality of the measurements before evaluation.

Visual fidelity:
Use approximately eight fixed cameras and four conditions. Record matching
reference/render pairs, field of view, time, exposure and quality preset.
Ask a local reviewer to identify incorrect architecture, signage and placement.

Geometry:
Test every intended entrance, stair, threshold and junction in the pilot.
Resolve visible floating meshes, gaps, blocking collision and fall-throughs.

Lighting:
Inspect interior/exterior transitions, window openings, thin surfaces,
reflections and moving-camera stability. Save diagnostic views where needed.

Weather:
Test exposed street, covered porch and indoor room. Rain/wetness should respect
the declared shelter behaviour. Wind direction should be coherent.

People:
Test task interruption, blocked navigation, reservation release, streaming
away/back and time-of-day changes.

Physics:
Repeat grab/drop/throw and resting-contact cases; inspect finite states,
penetration, tunnelling and persistence.

Streaming:
Walk/sprint the route, cross streaming boundaries, rotate rapidly, use the yard
teleport and enter/exit overview. Distant proxies must remain meaningful.

Performance:
Three repeatable runs plus a 30-minute session; save frame-time distributions,
memory peaks, largest stalls and settings. Report all target misses.

Delivery:
Launch the packaged build in a clean environment; verify dependencies,
attribution, generation inputs and regeneration behaviour.

The existing short smoke test remains useful for fast regression checks.
It should sit alongside these tests, not substitute for them.

15. FIRST TEN IMPLEMENTATION TASKS

1. Freeze the current input/build versions and capture a reproducible baseline.
2. Select the pilot street and define eight comparison viewpoints.
3. Audit actual height/floor/part attributes in the existing building extract.
4. Create the reference/confidence manifest and separate authored overrides.
5. Measure or obtain pilot ground profiles and road cross-sections.
6. Build one fully reviewed facade with correct depth, materials and collision.
7. Inspect that building's Lumen representations and interior daylight.
8. Expand it into a small regional kit and 12–20 distinct frontages.
9. Build HLODs and profile before adding a larger live population.
10. Add interiors, weather response, audio and routines, then package and
    validate the complete pilot.

16. FEATURES TO DEFER UNTIL THE PILOT WORKS

- Detailed interiors for all 821 shells.
- An always-active simulation for the entire population.
- Full-map real-time CFD or a claim of local weather prediction.
- Multiplayer, economies and large agent-training pipelines.
- Widespread 8K textures and maximum-quality rendering without measurement.
- An engine migration solely for novelty; stay with the documented 5.8.3
  baseline unless a specific verified capability or fix justifies a change.
- A full reconstruction based only on a single tour video.
- Neural captures or generated 3D assets as replacements for measured scale,
  editable geometry and verified collision.

Optional later research:
Compare a small measured street reconstruction against a procedural baseline
using reference fidelity, reconstruction effort, runtime cost and resident
recognition. A reproducible uncertainty-aware reconstruction workflow could
be a stronger project contribution than the number of plugins installed.
